Research

Advancing targeted insertion into plant genomes can mitigate the effects of climate change, enabling the production of resilient food crops and turning non-food crops into cost-effective platforms for biofuels, biomaterials, and biopharmaceuticals.

Traditional approaches for DNA insertion into plant genomes using Agrobacterium tumefaciens result in random integration, confounding measurements of gene expression, circuit behavior, and pathway output with position effects at the insertion site. Precise and efficient insertion of multi-kilobase DNA into predefined plant genomic loci remains one of the biggest challenges in plant engineering, with existing technologies limited by low efficiencies, unintended changes, off-target integration, and an inability to handle large payload sizes.

R2 retrotransposon-mediated DNA writing in plants

  • Engineered the avian Taeniopygia guttata R2 non-LTR retroelement (R2Tg) into a double-strand-break-free editor for targeted insertion of 2.2–5 kb DNA payloads at the 25S rDNA safe-harbor locus in N. benthamiana, A. thaliana, and S. lycopersicum, achieving insertion in up to 24% of transformed cells and approximately one copy per genome by ddPCR, with stable copy number across payload sizes.
  • Demonstrated 30-fold higher integration than Cas9-mediated homology-directed repair at the same locus.
  • Characterized integration specificity and epigenetic outcomes using long-read chromosome mapping, tagmentation-based integration-site sequencing, and targeted bisulfite sequencing, observing 96% on-target integration, precise 5′/3′ junctions, and unmethylated payloads in moderately methylated rDNA.

CRISPR-associated transposase (CAST)-mediated DNA insertion in plants

  • Adapted CRISPR-associated transposases from Vibrio cholerae (VchCAST) and Pseudoalteromonas (PseCAST) for targeted insertion in N. benthamiana leaves.
  • Reconstituted PACE-evolved Type I-F evoCAST in plants, reaching 8% insertion of a 1 kb promoter payload at a bidirectional reporter landing pad.

Retargeting R2 editors to new genomic loci

  • Extended R2 DNA writing beyond 25S rDNA by fusing the retroelement to SpCas9(H840A) nickase and reached 5% insertion by 5′-junction ddPCR at the 16C p35S-GFP locus in N. benthamiana.
  • Screened R2 fusions with enOgeuIscB, ISDra2 eTnpBc, Cas12a, and SaCas9 nickases using large guide RNA libraries targeting multiple loci in A. thaliana and N. benthamiana.

Developing heritable R2-mediated insertion in tomato and Arabidopsis thaliana

  • Discovered that constitutively active wild-type R2Tg is toxic to the Arabidopsis germline, with transformed seeds recovered only from reverse-transcriptase-dead and endonuclease-dead mutant lines.
  • Built R2-editor expression systems using inducible 17β-estradiol, dexamethasone, and methoxyfenozide promoters and developed the lab’s first successful Micro-Tom callus transformation protocol.
  • Validated that inducible control of R2Tg enables transformation in dividing cells and developed pipelines toward heritable R2-mediated editing with editor expression and stability across generations in Arabidopsis and tomato.

R2 delivery using nanoparticles

  • Delivered R2 editors via Photorhabdus virulence cassette (PVC) extracellular contractile injection system nanoparticles, observing mScarlet payload accumulation validating insertion events and measured 1.5% R2Tg integration after eCIS-mediated delivery.
